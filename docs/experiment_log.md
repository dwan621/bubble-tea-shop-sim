# Experiment Log

This is the public ledger of the study. It separates two kinds of evidence:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **re-runnable** | produced by the Python engine in this repository; anyone can reproduce it with a fixed command |
| **archival, not re-runnable** | produced manually in AnyLogic Personal Learning Edition, one seed per run; the model snapshot is kept under `archive/anylogic/` but needs proprietary AnyLogic to open, so its numbers are historical evidence only |

The archived AnyLogic screenshots live under
[`assets/slides/`](assets/slides/) and are referenced by relative path below. The
verbatim original Python scripts are kept under `legacy/` for provenance and are
superseded by the package.

> **中文**：本台账分两类证据：可重跑（Python 引擎，固定种子与命令）与“存档、不可重跑”
> （AnyLogic 手工单种子运行，模型快照不在仓库内）。存档数字只作历史证据与方向性对照，
> 不能当作引擎基准；可重跑的锚点由 `python -m bubble_tea_sim reproduce` 生成并被测试钉住。

## 1. Validity checklist

A run is logged only if every item passes. If a check fails, the run is excluded and the
cause is investigated first.

1. **Flow conservation.** Every generated customer is served or explicitly counted as
   balked/reneged; no customer is silently stuck in a queue.
   (`served + balked + reneged == generated`, checked in `tests/test_engine_invariants.py`.)
2. **Arrival proportions.** Realised class counts are consistent with the configured
   order mix within sampling noise.
3. **Utilisation check.** Observed utilisation is within ±10 percentage points of the
   analytical `λ p̄ / m` (the Python anchors are much closer: see the tables below).
4. **Complete configuration.** Workers, arrival rate, policy switches, run horizon and
   seed are recorded; a missing field means the run is not logged.
5. **Comparability.** Runs being paired differ only in the policy switch, with the same
   arrival stream and horizon.
6. **Directionality.** Fast priority must not worsen fast-item LOS; a violation is a
   red flag, not a result.
7. **Economic consistency.** Revenue equals served orders × class prices; counts are
   conserved; profit can be recomputed from revenue, wage and waiting cost.
8. **Archive label.** Any manually collected AnyLogic number is tagged
   *archival, not re-runnable* and is never used as a benchmark for the engine's absolute
   values.

> **中文**：录入前逐项核对流量守恒、到达比例、利用率偏差（≤±10pp）、配置完整、
> 对照可比、方向性、经济口径，以及存档标签。任何一项不通过都不入账。

## 4. v2 engine re-check of the archive

The archive cannot be re-run in AnyLogic, but it can be re-checked with the engine in two
ways: (a) a machine-generated ledger that re-implements each E-series run at its
archived settings, and (b) hand-checks of the A/B/C/P-series anchors. Neither changes
the archive; both quantify how far the shipped engine is from it.

### 4.1 E-series ledger (machine-generated, authoritative)

```powershell
python analysis/reproduce_anylogic_ledger.py     # -> results/anylogic_ledger.json
```

The script runs the archived configurations (3 clerks, 1000 minutes, no warm-up, seed 1)
with 200 replicates and compares the engine mean with the archived profit per hour. The
tolerances are 5% at λ = 1.00 and 12% at λ = 1.25.

| Run | Archived profit/h | Engine profit/h (200-rep mean) | Deviation | Within tolerance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| E1 | 292.917 | 303.00 | +3.4% | yes |
| E2 | 295.906 | 304.81 | +3.0% | yes |
| E3 | 349.478 | 323.28 | −7.5% | yes |
| E4 | 349.184 | 319.17 | −8.6% | yes |
| E5 | 358.359 | 337.71 | −5.8% | yes |
| E6 | 359.161 | 335.14 | −6.7% | yes |

Verdict from `results/anylogic_ledger.json`: **passed**. Low load 2/2 and high load 4/4
within tolerance; the fast-priority runs (E5, E6) are the two most profitable in both
worlds; the ranking matches the archive except that E5 and E6 swap places (archive
E6 > E5 by 0.8 per hour, engine E5 > E6 by 2.6 per hour — small enough to be run-to-run
noise); and the split−synchro utilisation difference is **exactly 0.0** at both loads,
as the capacity argument requires.

What the match does *not* mean:

* Matching profit is not matching times. At λ = 1.25 the engine's LOS is 25–43% higher
  than the archive (E3: tea 5.16 vs 4.11; E4: bread 3.61 vs 2.53). The engine also
  serves slightly more customers (1254 vs 1227) and books ~3% more revenue, and the
  waiting-cost weight (0.5 per customer-minute) is a modest share of hourly revenue, so
  profit stays inside tolerance while individual LOS values do not.
* The archived utilisation display is not the engine's utilisation: the archive shows
  67% / 84%, while its own served counts imply 68.8% / 86.6% (a 1030-minute measurement
  window instead of the nominal 1000), and the engine reports 70.8% / 88.5%.
* **Archive integrity flag.** E3 is the one row whose numbers cannot all be true
  simultaneously: applying the documented profit formula to its own `revenueTotal`
  (`9346`), `flowTimeTotal` (`4949.015`) and wage gives 337.44 per hour, not the
  archived 349.478 (−3.4%). The other five rows reconcile within 0.03%. This is a
  data-recording issue in the archive, not an engine result, and it is the honest reason
  E3's deviation looks worse than E4's.

> **中文**：机器台账以 AnyLogic 的 1000 分钟、无预热、seed 1 设置复算 E1–E6：
> 利润偏差为 +3.4% ~ −8.6%，全部落在 5%/12% 容差内；快品优先仍是最赚钱的两组，
> 仅 E5/E6 排序互换；Split−Synchro 利用率差精确为 0。利润接近不代表 LOS 接近：
> λ=1.25 时引擎 LOS 高 25–43%。另外 E3 是唯一一行“收入/flow/利润”三者互相矛盾的
> 存档记录（按其自身字段复算利润少 3.4%），属于存档记录问题，需在引用时注明。

Engine values are the re-runnable P-series and mixed-order anchors from Section 2

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 vs A2 (λ=1.0) | P-series, fast vs FCFS | 0.77 → 0.89; FCFS 0.90 → 1.04 | 2.83 → 2.95; 2.81 → 2.92 | bread −14.4% vs −14.4% | direction/magnitude agree |
| B2 vs B1 (λ=1.3) | P-series, fast vs FCFS | 1.04 → 1.11; FCFS 1.71 → 1.88 | 3.20 → 3.85; 3.13 → 3.70 | bread −39% vs −41% | direction/magnitude agree; tea gap is at the ±20% archive tolerance |
| C1 (λ=1.0, ice branch) | P-series, FCFS, ice 15% | 1.04 → 1.04 (bread) | 2.91 → 2.92 | ice: 1.27 → 1.22 | near-exact agreement |
| B3/B4 (λ=1.5) | P-series, FCFS/fast | 2.42 → 4.34; 1.11 → 1.32 | 4.51 → 6.21; 4.62 → 6.70 | bread −54% vs −70% | **not comparable in absolute terms**: archive util 86% vs engine 93.6% implies a different effective load/parameter lineage; direction still matches |
| M3-A (λ=1.0, Split) | legacy mixed-order split **+ bread priority** | 0.91 → 0.945 (+3.8%) | normal tea 3.12 → 3.305 (+5.9%); mixed tea 2.84 → 3.16 (+11%) | ice hand-off 1.23 → 1.10 (−10%) | component pattern agrees; the archive's bread LOS only matches when bread priority is on, so that switch is ambiguous in the archived label |
| M3-B (λ=1.0, Synchro) | legacy mixed-order synchro, FCFS | 1.40 → 1.428 (+2.0%) | normal tea 3.06 → 3.252 (+6.3%); mixed sync 3.40 → 3.581 (+5.3%) | — | agrees within 6% |
| M3-C/D (λ=1.25) | legacy mixed-order split/synchro | bread 2.53/2.43 vs engine 3.72/3.49 (+47%/+44%) | normal tea 4.23/4.11 vs engine 5.52/5.31 (+31%/+29%) | — | **not comparable**: archive util 84% vs engine 88.4% (same load-lineage problem as B3/B4); structural evidence only |

The important takeaways from the re-check:

1. The engine reproduces the archived **FCFS baseline** and the **relative** fast-priority
   effect; the C-series three-class baseline is nearly exact.
2. Archived **absolute** LOS at high load (B3/B4, M3-C/D) cannot be reproduced: the
   archived utilisation (86% / 84%) is inconsistent with the Python parameterisation
   (93.6% / 88.4%), so those runs are historical evidence, not a benchmark.
3. The M3 mixed-order archive is a structurally different (nested) model; the engine
   reproduces the qualitative pattern (early ice hand-off; mixed customer leaves with the
   tea), not the exact component values.
4. The E-series ledger is the only comparison run at exactly the archived settings; its
   verdict (profit within tolerance, ranking nearly matched) is the authoritative
   cross-model check. The generated tables live in `docs/experiments.md`.

> **中文**：A/B/C 手工复算显示：FCFS 基线与快品优先的相对效应可复现（−14% vs −14%、
> −39% vs −41%），C 系列三品类基线几乎重合；B3/B4 与 M3-C/D 的绝对 LOS 不可复现，
> 因为存档利用率（86%/84%）与 Python 参数化（93.6%/88.4%）不一致。E 系列机器台账
> 是按存档设置复算的唯一权威交叉验证，见 4.1 与 `results/anylogic_ledger.json`。

### 6.2 Validity and calibration notes

* **Layer-off equivalence.** `baseline_flat` (no v2 layer) reproduces the v1 configuration
  bit-for-bit. Pinned by `tests/test_v2_layers.py::test_baseline_flat_scenario_is_bit_exact_v1`
  and re-checked by `analysis/build_report_v2.py` on every report build (it prints the
  result rather than asserting it silently).
* **Balk calibration (v2 fix).** The balking estimate divides the expected in-system work
  by the number of **staff units on duty at that instant**, not by nominal headcount, so a
  break or a shift boundary genuinely reduces the shop's perceived capacity. This changed
  the demand and economics numbers relative to the first v2 draft; the files listed above
  were regenerated after the fix and are the authoritative versions.
* **SLA fairness alias (v2 fix).** `sla.fairness_cap_minutes` is now mapped into
  `policy.starvation_cap` by the scenario loader (an explicit `policy.starvation_cap` wins),
  so scenarios such as `strict_sla_fairness` really enable the cap. Covered by the v2
  layer tests.
* **Flow conservation and finiteness.** Every scatter in the scenario catalogue records
  `validation.flow_conservation` and `validation.all_metrics_finite`; all 16 pass, and the
  report re-runs the conservation check for every scenario at build time.
* **AnyLogic still archival.** None of the v2 numbers depend on the archived AnyLogic
  runs; the E-series ledger in Section 4 remains the only cross-check of the archive and
  is still not a re-runnable artifact.
